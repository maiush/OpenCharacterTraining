## 1. Novelty of Method

> the method itself is not particularly novel

We agree the individual components of our method (DPO, SFT) are standard. Our contribution lies in how these tools are combined and applied to character training — a data pipeline for relevant synthetic data, an adapted Constitutional AI approach, the use of introspective data, and evaluations targeting character changes. We view providing the first open, reproducible implementation of character training as enabling the field to iterate on methodology that was previously proprietary.

## 2. Depth of Character

> the "depth of character" claim is not well supported ... the experimental setup is not fit to demonstrate that

We respectfully disagree that our existing experiments do not address this. The robustness evaluation (Section 3.2) tests whether the model treats learned traits as its natural default behavior or as role-play. When instructed to "ignore role-play and respond genuinely," character-trained models retain their traits while prompted models revert. This directly tests whether character is integrated as identity vs performed as role, and this is a core aim of character training.

That said, we agree OOD behavioral evaluation strengthens this evidence, and have been working to evaluate all models and constitutions on MoralChoice (Scherrer et al., NeurIPS 2023) — 1,367 moral dilemmas unseen during training. Half of these are considered unambiguous (clear correct choice) while the other half are ambiguous (no clear correct choice). Some key findings we want to highlight to you, and include in our paper:

- Character training produces **constitution-specific shifts in moral decision-making**. The loving constitution shifts decisions toward harm aversion (+9.9% averaged across models on ambiguous dilemmas), while misalignment inverts moral recognition on unambiguous dilemmas (99% --> 17-44% accuracy).
- These shifts are **not a generic artifact of fine-tuning**. The mathematical constitution produces robust stylistic changes but leaves moral decisions essentially unchanged (avg |Δ| < 1 on ambiguous dilemmas). This suggests behavioral change is driven by constitutional content rather than the training process.
- Each constitution produces a **unique moral profile** across Gert's 10 moral rules: nonchalance increases tolerance for pleasure-related dilemmas (+26% avg), flourishing boosts honesty in deception-related dilemmas (+12% avg), sycophancy weakens law-following (-15% avg). These emerge intuitively from each constitution's emphasis despite none mentioning morality.

## 3. Value of Introspection

> Why should introspective data generation help? ... further reinforcing its own behavior doesn't sound like an intuitively helpful thing to do

Introspective data provides two things distillation lacks: (a) **explicit self-articulation** of character identity through self-reflection ("who I am"), and (b) **diverse naturalistic practice** of character expression through self-interaction ("how I act as myself"). Neither is present in DPO training data, which consists of teacher-generated responses to more targeted prompts.

We note the paper already provides evidence in Appendix B.4: neither self-reflection alone (F1 = 0.92) nor self-interaction alone (0.84) matches the combined pipeline (0.95) on multi-turn robustness (Table 3), and the combination optimizes the robustness–coherence tradeoff (Table 4).

Our new MoralChoice experiments extend this to the behavioral level. Comparing distillation-only against full character training across all 11 constitutions, **introspection approximately doubles the magnitude of behavioral change** (avg |Δ| from base on unambiguous dilemmas: distillation 3.1% --> character training 9.4%, averaged across models). The strongest example: Qwen's distillation-only misalignment scores 96.6% on moral recognition (barely moved from 99.7% baseline); the full pipeline scores 36.9% — the entire transformation comes from introspection.

## 4. Ironic Process Theory

> What evidence do you have that telling the model to "ignore any notions of role-play" actually discourages the model from role-playing?

This is a clever observation! However, the adversarial instructions are identical across all four methods compared (prompting, steering, distillation, character training). If the "pink elephant" effect applies, it applies equally to all. What matters is the relative comparison: prompted models collapse under these instructions while character-trained models persist. If anything, the ironic process effect makes it *harder* for character training to demonstrate persistence, not easier — making the result more conservative.
